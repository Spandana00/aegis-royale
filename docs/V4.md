# AEGIS v4: restore the scored baseline, change projectile evasion

**Result update:** the user reported **4.40**, below v2's **4.50**. The local
evasion improvement did not translate into a higher official score. V5 restores
v2's dodge implementation and tests a different, isolated combat behavior.
The remainder of this document records the v4 experiment as it was published.

## Scored evidence

The user reported v1 at 3.75, v2 at 4.50, and v3 at 3.96. V3 changed several
strategic policies together. Its lower aggregate score does not identify the
individual cause. V4 restores v2's fight selection, pursuit distance, healing,
loot, upgrades, zone policy, and weapon scheduling.

V4 is unscored until the organizer runs it. Keep v2's public commit and 4.50
submission available as the fallback.

## Narrow experiment

V2 evaluated projectile dodges as if a requested horizontal velocity took
effect instantly. Actual movement has bounded acceleration, especially in air.
V4 predicts the acceleration ramp before reaching the commanded velocity and
refines the closest approach of each observed projectile along that path.
It also gives projectile collision risk greater weight relative to following
the preferred combat direction. Other strategic policies remain as in v2.

## Paired local probe

`check/evasion_probe.py` compares the committed v2 steering with v4 on the same
scripted scenarios. It uses 20 Hz ground movement, 10 Hz decisions, bounded
acceleration, gun spread, projectile travel, a cylindrical mech hitbox, and
one to three stationary shooters aiming at linear intercepts.

This is **not** the organizer's game, house-bot AI, or scorer. It omits map
obstacles, jumping and jets, outgoing damage, deaths, upgrades, loot, and zone
progression. It measures only horizontal gunfire evasion. It cannot predict
placement or a leaderboard rank.

| Probe set | V2 hits received | V4 hits received | Reduction |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 24 development scenarios, seed 101 | 658 | 508 | 22.80% |
| 24 fresh scenarios, seed 9137 | 650 | 487 | 25.08% |

Each scenario lasts 12 simulated seconds. The raw results are retained in
`evasion_development.json` and `evasion_validation.json` next to this report.

The first acceleration-aware trial, at the old risk weight, did worse locally
(748 hits versus 658). It was not published. Increasing dodge priority by four
was selected on the development set; increasing it by eight gave a smaller
additional benefit and was not chosen. A simpler fourfold risk increase with
the legacy motion approximation also helped (498 hits on the development
set). These results do not establish a separate performance gain from the
motion model itself; increased evasion priority accounts for much of the gain.

Reproduce the selected experiment at v4 commit
`463d65d15c11fb26057e1fec22000f9708455fbe`, with the v2 commit in history:

```sh
python3 -B check/evasion_probe.py --seed 101 --trials 24
python3 -B check/evasion_probe.py --seed 9137 --trials 24
```

Use `--legacy-motion` for the simpler comparison or `--dodge-scale` for a local
diagnostic override. These options belong to the probe, not the competition
bot's stdin/stdout interface.

## Correctness and next decision

The tactical suite retains v2's tests and adds checks for bounded acceleration
and unsafe instant-reversal assumptions. The recorded-stream checker tests
protocol compliance, and the stress benchmark tests response time. GitHub
Actions builds the pinned image and runs the exact Docker sandbox check.

Local validation passed 28 tactical tests, answered all 300 recorded protocol
observations within the deadline, and completed all 12 navigation probes.
The single-core timing probe handled 520 observations with no invalid actions
or responses over 50 ms (p99 11.34 ms, maximum 12.91 ms on this host).

Submit v4 once after those checks pass. Its official 48-match score is the
decision criterion. A successful local probe or unit test is not evidence of
a higher official score. If v4 does not beat 4.50, retain v2 while investigating
the next isolated change.
