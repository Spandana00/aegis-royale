## Reproduced bug

V2 extrapolates a target's velocity, applies gravity, then clamps the aim point
to at least one meter below the target's **current** feet. A rapidly descending
mech can be below that point by the time the bullet reaches it.

For example, a target 100 m away with feet at 12 m and downward speed 24 m/s
lands before the bullet arrives. V2's clamp aims at height 11 m. The landed
body spans 0–9 m, so the shot misses overhead. V6 aims at height 4.68 m, inside
the landed torso. This is a projectile prediction error, not a claim about
what caused any particular official-match death.

## Fix and scope

For descending mechs, V6 predicts feet separately from body center. It uses
gravity or recent observed vertical acceleration, accounts for the barrel's
forward offset when estimating flight time, and iterates the intercept.
The predicted feet stop at the landing surface; the aim point is then placed
inside the cube-scaled body.

Landing height includes terrain and solid prism tops below the possible
landing height. Bridge roofs are not mistaken for floors when shooting at a
mech below them, and courtyard holes do not become artificial floors. Recent
observations can identify jet braking; implausibly large velocity jumps from
landings or impacts are not extrapolated as sustained acceleration. Samples
expire after a short gap.

Gun predictions for targets that are not descending retain v2's calculation.
Other weapons and the shared-aim firing scheduler are unchanged. Horizontal
motion is still extrapolated linearly, and future changes in jet input,
collisions, overhang interactions, and enemy steering remain uncertain.

## Controlled collision check

`check/gun_prediction_probe.py` compares v2 and v6 against independently
evaluated target trajectories and a swept gun ray. It covers four distances,
three lateral speeds, two cube sizes, multiple fall heights and speeds,
ground landings, roof landings, rising targets, and observed jet braking.
It samples vertical overlap at sub-millisecond spacing during the horizontal
cylinder crossing and checks solid geometry on the shot path.

| Scripted group | Cases | V2 hits | V6 hits |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Grounded | 24 | 24 | 24 |
| Rising | 144 | 144 | 144 |
| Falling to ground | 288 | 216 | 288 |
| Falling onto a roof | 216 | 180 | 216 |
| Jet braking during descent | 144 | 144 | 144 |
| Total | 816 | 708 | 816 |

There were 108 corrected misses and no hit regressions in this scripted matrix.
Grounded and rising firing solutions were exactly equal between versions.
The raw summary and examples are retained in `gun_prediction_validation.json`.

This is **not the game, house-bot AI, or placement scorer**. It isolates aiming
with known acceleration and landing surfaces. It omits spread, torso slew,
enemy decisions, health, kills, and strategic interaction. Its 816 successful
candidate rays do not imply perfect accuracy in a real match or a particular
leaderboard score.

Reproduce from a clone containing v2's submitted commit:

```sh
python3 -B check/gun_prediction_probe.py
```
